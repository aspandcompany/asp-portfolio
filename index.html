ASP & Co. redesigns and reframes how people engage with places.

Every place has a lived reality that most people never encounter — not because it's hidden, but because nobody built a path to it. We build that path. Through research, sustained presence, and honest visual storytelling, we close the distance between the surface of a place and its truth — so that everyone who encounters it, whether they just arrived or have lived there their whole lives, can engage with it more fully, more honestly, and more meaningfully.

For the people who live there. For the people who arrive. And for the brands that bring them.

Who We Work With

Five conversations.
One research posture.

ASP & Co. documents the lived experience of place — the culture, the community, and the environment that exist alongside the visitor-facing identity every destination already has. What we build is a more complete picture: the story travelers know, and the one they haven't heard yet, documented together.

Destinations DMOs, tourism boards, CVBs +

A destination's official identity is usually built around what draws visitors — the landmarks, the experiences, the things most people associate with a place before they arrive. That foundation is real, and ASP & Co. documents it with the same craft and rigor applied to everything else. But a place is more than its visitor-facing identity. It is also the surf culture that has defined a stretch of coastline for generations. The founding story that never made it into the brochure. The neighborhood that shaped the city's music before anyone was paying attention.

ASP & Co. works with destinations to build a visual record that holds both: the identity travelers already know and the lived culture that exists alongside it. A brochure is not a place. ASP & Co. documents what the brochure left out.

Travel & Hospitality Operators, cruise lines, hotels & resorts +

The most memorable travel experiences aren't the ones that show guests the most polished version of a place. They're the ones that show them something true about it. Editorial Regional culture, luxury travel, and diaspora travel publications +

Place-based storytelling is easy to get wrong. A weekend visit produces a weekend's worth of observation. What editors and readers can sense — even when they can't name it — is the difference between a piece written from the outside and one written from sustained presence inside a place.

ASP & Co. brings a documented research methodology, a proprietary archive built from years of field work across the American coastal South, and a geographic thesis to every editorial relationship: that the places worth writing about are the ones where the lived culture runs deeper than the visitor experience, and where the two exist alongside each other in ways that reward careful attention. A weekend produces observation, not perspective. ASP & Co. brings the years of presence that create it.

Cultural & Conservation Heritage organizations, conservation nonprofits, foundations & museums +

Conservation has always understood that what isn't documented is at risk. The same is true for culture. The surf shops that have been on a barrier island for forty years. The congregation whose music shaped a city's identity. The farming community whose relationship to the land predates the development that's now erasing it. These aren't marginal stories. They are the living record of what a place is — and without documentation, they disappear.

ASP & Co. works with institutions whose mission is preservation to build visual records of the communities, traditions, and environments in their care. The research methodology is the same applied to every geography: document the physical environment, document the cultural life that grew out of it, document the present-day condition honestly. An archive isn't preservation until it's public. ASP & Co. gives the culture a record before it disappears.

Academic & Scientific Universities, marine science institutions, research organizations +

Research that doesn't reach the public it's meant to serve is research with an incomplete impact. The science exists — the ecological surveys, the marine assessments, the environmental field work — but the translation layer between that knowledge and the communities it affects is often thin, generic, or missing entirely. A report is not a story. Data is not a relationship with a place.

ASP & Co. works at the intersection of scientific fieldwork and visual storytelling — documenting research in the field with the same depth brought to cultural documentation, and translating that work into imagery and narrative that a general audience can connect to. The Worldview

Creation Research.
From Higher Ground.

Creation research is the act of studying geography and place as living and breathing creation — approaching the coastlines, communities, and environments we enter with the reverence and rigor they deserve, because the places we document are not backdrop. They are alive. And what is alive is worth keeping.

This is the heartbeat of ASP & Co. — rooted in the conviction that geography and place carry meaning that rewards sustained, careful attention. Not the attention of a passing observer, but the attention of someone who believes the earth itself is worth studying the way one studies anything sacred.

We are lovers of geography. That love is not passive. It produces a discipline: the practice of going somewhere, paying deep attention, and asking what this place is trying to say — before a single frame is made, before a portfolio is built, before an experience is designed.

The watchtower is the vantage point. Creation research is the practice that happens from it.

What drives the work

01

Belonging is a credential.

The most important thing a photographer can bring to a place is time already spent there. Not access. Not equipment. Presence — the kind that makes you invisible enough to see what's actually there.

02

Document before it changes.

Every place is mid-negotiation between what it was and what it's becoming. The work of documentation is to catch that moment — not to mourn it, but to make sure there's a record of it.

03

The archive is the product.

A single shoot produces a single moment. An archive produces a relationship — with a place, with its people, with the light at different times of year. That's what's actually valuable to license.

04

Stories over surfaces.

The best destination imagery doesn't show what a place looks like. It shows what it feels like to belong there. That's a harder thing to capture — and the only thing worth trying for.

About

The 225 Lens.

ASP & Co.'s four-movement research methodology for entering a place, recovering its documented truth, and returning it as a Visual Reckoning, a Written Reckoning, and a Physical Reckoning.

The Methodology

One method. Four movements.

"I will restore to you the years the locusts have eaten." — Joel 2:25

I

Movement One

The Physical Record

Intensive study and visual documentation of the natural environment — waterways, geography, ecology, the built landscape. The land and water as they actually exist, before any story is told about them.

II

Movement Two

The Cultural Intersection

Equally intensive study of the cultural heritage and human livelihoods native to that environment — the economies, traditions, and communities that grew directly out of the physical record. This is where the relationship between people and place gets defined.

III

Movement Three

The Lived Reality

The present-day condition. What of that culture survives, what has been commodified, what has been displaced. The gap between how a place markets itself and how its people actually live inside it now.

IV

Movement Four

The Audit and the Gap

The written reckoning. An honest critique of what lifestyle branding has consistently left out — whose story has stopped renewing, whose identity has been frozen in archive while others circulate freely. The demand is not to abandon the form. It is to apply it fully.

The 225 Lens is a continuous loop — each trip through the field feeds the next research cycle, and the archive compounds with every project.

The Tarpon Atlas.

Tarpon Springs is one of the most culturally layered coastal communities in the American coastal South — and one of the least accurately documented. Greek sponge diving heritage and Bahamian cultural roots exist in the same city, separated by geography but connected by water. This field record documents both, from the inside.

Sponge Docks Rose Cemetery Fred Howard Park

Tarpon Springs, FL — Anclote River · 28.1486° N, 82.7537° W

Sponge Docks & Spring Bayou

The story of the Tarpon Springs sponge docks begins with the Bahamians — Black sponge fishermen who settled here in the late 1800s and introduced the traditional hook method, working the Gulf beds before anyone else had a commercial operation. The Greeks arrived in the early 1900s, bringing helmet diving and a more industrial approach that transformed the trade. Both communities built this place. The docks carry both histories, though one has been told louder than the other.

Tarpon Springs, FL — North Jasmine Ave · 28.1481° N, 82.7342° W

Rose Cemetery & Tarpon's Bahamian Heritage

Rose Cemetery — also known as Rose Hill Cemetery — is the oldest and largest intact segregated African-American cemetery in Pinellas County, established in the early 1900s on North Jasmine Avenue. The Bahamian community buried here includes the hook spongers who built the trade before the Greeks arrived. The graves tell the story plainly: conch shells from Eleuthera left on headstones, east-west burial orientation carried over from African and Caribbean tradition. This is where Black Tarpon Springs keeps its record.

Fred Howard Park & Sunset Beach

The Gulf-facing side of Tarpon Springs — a causeway park that ends in a beach that hasn't been developed past its own good sense. Fred Howard Park is one of the better-kept coastal parks in Pinellas County, and Sunset Beach sits just south, a small residential strip with water on both sides. The light at the end of the causeway at dusk is something specific to this place.

The Work

The Origin Brief.

Every place has a cultural origin. Most businesses built in that place have never been formally introduced to it.

01 · The Foundation

The Origin Brief is a strategic consulting document that answers three questions for any place-based business.

  • 01

    What is the cultural and geographical foundation this place is actually built on — the waterways, communities, migrations, and traditions that shaped it before your business arrived?

  • 02

    Where is the gap between how your business currently represents itself and the deeper story it could be telling?

  • 03

    How can your photography, visual storytelling, social media, web presence, and brand expression be reoriented around that origin — so that what you put into the world reflects where you actually are?

02 · How to Engage

Two ways to engage.

The Brief is built from ASP & Co.'s place-based research. It is not a rebrand. It is a deepening — a formal introduction between a business and the place it calls home.

Consulting

ASP & Co. researches, writes, and delivers the Origin Brief. Your team executes.

Consulting + Visual Portfolio

The same Brief, with visual content from ASP & Co.'s Archive or commissioned specifically for your location. For locations not yet in the Archive, ASP & Co. builds a Commissioned Place Portfolio — entering the location, conducting the research, and producing place-based visual content that no stock library can replicate.

To inquire about an Origin Brief for your business, reach out through the contact page.

The Work

Rooted Travel Partnerships.

We help travel brands build deeper relationships between visitors and the places they serve.

Travel as relationship-building — not just experience collecting.

01 · The Approach

The most compelling travel experiences are not the ones that look the most polished. They are the ones that know where they are.

ASP & Co. partners with experiential travel operators — cruise lines, tour companies, luxury travel brands — who want their experiences to be genuinely rooted in the cultural identity of the places they operate in.

The work is embedded. ASP & Co. travels with or alongside an operator, documenting the actual interaction between the experiences being sold and the places where they happen. The result is field photography, written findings, and a strategic direction built from what the research found — not from what the marketing already assumed.

"A guest who understands the place they're visiting doesn't just have a better trip. They become an advocate for it — and they come back."

Most travel content produces a recognizable result: polished, generic, and interchangeable. The content looks the same because the people making it do not stay long enough, go deep enough, or know enough about the place to make anything else. ASP & Co. is the opposite of that model. The research always comes before the frame.

What Operators Bring

Existing experiences in specific places.

A genuine desire to go deeper than the standard shore excursion or curated itinerary. Openness to honest findings about what the experience is — and what it could be.

What ASP & Co. Brings

The 225 Lens applied to travel.

The same research methodology used across every Identity Portfolio in the Archive, now directed at how an operator's experiences interact with place identity, culture, and history. Not audit language. Research language.

02 · How It Works

The research keeps renewing.

The underlying research archive produced during every partnership belongs to ASP & Co. and feeds the broader Archive. Operators license the documentary content produced for their specific use.

ASP & Co. is actively building partnerships with operators who are already doing meaningful work in place and want to go further. The goal on both sides is the same: visitors who leave knowing where they've been.

Place-based visual content is available to businesses, publications, DMOs, and hospitality brands in two ways. One draws from what already exists in the Archive. The other builds something new.

The Visual Reckoning is available for licensing at two levels.

Record imagery goes deeper — into the cultural heritage, lived reality, and the stories a place's reputation has left out. This imagery is released for licensing only after that Record's Reckoning of Place is complete and published. As each Reckoning is finished, the licensing parameters for that Portfolio expand.

About

Press & media.

The Founder

Ashton Stepter is the founder of ASP & Co., a creation research practice documenting the intersection of cultural heritage and environmental significance across the American coastal South. The work began in 2019 on a barrier island in North Carolina and has since expanded through St. Petersburg, Florida, where the practice is now headquartered. Stepter identifies as a cultural scientist — approaching geography and place as living subjects worth studying with rigor and reverence before a single frame is made. He is the author of the 225 Lens, ASP & Co.'s four-movement research methodology for entering a place, recovering its documented truth, and returning it as a Visual Reckoning, a Written Reckoning, and a Physical Reckoning. He works best with people who have something real at stake in how their place is understood — not those simply looking for content.

Press and interview requests — ashton@aspandco.com

The Practice

ASP & Co. is a creation research practice that documents the intersection of cultural heritage and environmental significance in specific places across the American coastal South and Atlantic world. Every engagement follows the 225 Lens, a four-movement research methodology which resolves into three deliverables — a Visual Reckoning, a Written Reckoning, and a Physical Reckoning. The result is primary-source documentation of places and the people who define them, produced by a practice that is present in the field long before a camera comes out.
